0. Executive Snapshot

Bottom Line

SPARTA is a credible strategic control point for Airbus inside FCAS, addressing a gap (mission-aware cyber trust at the edge) that none of the publicly disclosed FCAS pillars own end-to-end. It is technically aligned with Airbus's Combat Cloud (Pillar 4) and Remote Carriers (Pillar 3) responsibilities F, and reinforces Airbus's open-architecture (MARS) thesis I. Recommendation: GO, with conditions.

Why the panel will challenge it
  • Cyber overlap risk with Indra (Pillar 5 Sensors / cyber heritage) and Thales — political sensitivity.
  • Authority boundary (advisory vs actuation) must be airtight to be acceptable to Dassault and operators.
  • Numerical performance values are still provisional at PDR (see Deliverable 02 §10).
  • Combat Cloud architecture commitments at FCAS programme level are not yet frozen A.

1.3 Industrial & Political Constraints

Structural Constraints
  • Workshare politics: Each pillar is partitioned by nation/prime; any new capability framed as a new pillar would re-open the workshare negotiation that ran 18+ months in 2021–2022 [R-14][R-20][R-21][R-27] F.
  • IP / data rights: The 2022 Trappier–Schoellhorn framework explicitly addressed background IP and "best-athlete" principles; Eric Trappier's public statements on IP (2021–2022) are the canonical expression [R-14][R-28][R-29] F.
  • Sovereign cyber: Cyber capabilities tend to be national-sovereign — DGA-MI (France), BSI & Cyber-Innovationshub (Germany), CCN-CERT & INCIBE (Spain); industrial cyber products must accommodate sovereign overlays rather than replace them [R-06][R-09][R-35] I.
  • Coalition tension with GCAP: The UK/Italy/Japan Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP) trilateral convention was signed 14 December 2023, creating a structural competitor for partners and exports [R-37][R-30] F.
  • Decision rhythm: Funding is voted in annual / biennial slices in each parliament (French PLF, German Bundeshaushalt, Spanish PGE); programme commitments must survive German Haushaltsausschuss scrutiny in particular [R-06][R-07][R-08][R-09] F.
  • Competing US CCA programme: US Air Force Collaborative Combat Aircraft (Increment 1) contracts awarded April 2024 to Anduril and General Atomics set a parallel benchmark on loyal-wingman class [R-38] F.

3. Role of MARS & TSOC

3.1 MARS — Open Mission System Architecture

MARS is Airbus DS's published open, modular, model-based reference architecture for mission systems on combat-cloud-enabled platforms F. Its strategic effect is to convert integration from a one-off NRE cost into a repeatable plug-in pattern:

  • Standardized data contracts and APIs for mission, trust, and effector functions reduce per-platform integration cost (estimated 30–60% by analogy with avionic open-architecture programs such as FACE in the US A).
  • Plug-and-fly third-party modules become possible — including SPARTA — without re-opening the platform's mission system core I.
  • Open in form, controlled in substance: the schema, governance, and reference implementation are owned by Airbus, which is the actual control point I.

3.2 TSOC — Tactical SOC / Operational Range

The Tactical Security Operations Centre is positioned in the SPARTA baseline as the upward authority for cyber adjudication, policy, and evidence (Deliverable 01 §6, Deliverable 03 §8). Strategically, TSOC is also:

  • Training & mission-rehearsal environment for cyber-trust scenarios under representative threats I.
  • Validation environment for new content (rules, models, policies) before operational release (Deliverable 04 §11.4).
  • Operator immersion point that turns SPARTA outputs into operator-ready intuition — where customers form their long-term sourcing preferences I.

3.3 Why MARS & TSOC are Strategic Control Points

Control-Point Logic
  1. Architecture authority: Whoever owns MARS owns the data contracts the rest of the SoS must conform to. That is structural pricing power I.
  2. Operator-side gravity: Whoever owns the TSOC owns the analyst workflows. Analyst workflow lock-in is one of the stickiest forms of customer dependency in defence cyber I.
  3. Recurring revenue: Both MARS and TSOC generate service revenue (content updates, threat models, policy bundles, training) on a faster cycle than the platform itself I.

5. Killer Value Proposition

Killer Value Proposition (Airbus internal use)

"SPARTA is the cyber-trust spine of the FCAS Combat Cloud. By owning the trust object that every node, sensor and Remote Carrier emits and consumes, Airbus locks in the data contract on which Combat Cloud value depends — and converts a one-shot platform sale into a recurring trust-content service across FCAS, Eurofighter, Eurodrone and partner fleets."

QuestionAnswer
Why should Airbus invest?Because Combat Cloud without trust is operationally indefensible — and the customer will eventually demand a trust layer. Better Airbus delivers it than a third party defines it.
What strategic advantage does it create?Architectural ownership of the trust object inside MARS = pricing power across the FCAS lifecycle and structural lock-in vs Dassault and external cyber primes.
Why can't competitors easily replicate it?Replication requires simultaneously (a) Combat Cloud prime status, (b) MARS data-model authority, (c) operational mission-context access via MARS interfaces, and (d) TSOC analyst workflow ownership. Airbus alone holds all four positions in FCAS.

Authority chain remains C2 → MARS → SPARTA-as-advisor. Final accreditation envelope for Level-1 protections is still TBC at PDR.

14. Final Recommendation

Decision: GO — with binding conditions

SPARTA addresses a real, structural gap in the FCAS Combat Cloud value chain that no other programme element currently owns. It is uniquely well-positioned for Airbus to lead because the four positions required — Combat Cloud prime, MARS architecture authority, mission-context interface, and TSOC analyst-workflow control — are all simultaneously held by Airbus DS within FCAS. The investment is moderate, the strategic-control upside is high, and the political risk is manageable with disciplined framing.

14.1 Key Reasons

  1. Architectural ownership: Owning the trust object inside MARS turns Combat Cloud from connectivity into trustable mission fabric — and locks in pricing power across the FCAS lifecycle.
  2. Defensible competitive moat: Replication requires the simultaneous combination of CC primacy + MARS authority + mission-context access + TSOC ownership. No competitor has all four.
  3. Reuse leverage: Trust object reuses across Eurofighter LTE, Eurodrone, MRTT, helicopters and coalition C2 — multiplying programme-level value.
  4. Schema timing: The MARS schema window is the binding decision date. Failing to act before schema freeze surrenders the option permanently.
  5. Risk management: Authority partition (SYS-DC-001), advisory-only response, signed content, sovereign overlays — all designed-in to survive accreditation, sovereignty, and political objections.

14.2 Binding Conditions for GO

  1. Written workshare positioning of SPARTA inside Pillar 4 (Combat Cloud).
  2. Letter of intent from FCAS Combat Cloud architecture board to fold SPARTA data objects into MARS canonical schema before freeze.
  3. Joint TSOC-adjudication demonstration plan with Indra to neutralize sovereignty pushback.
  4. Airbus DS Finance-confirmed revenue model and investment envelope.
  5. Closure of provisional performance values on one representative node-class HIL.

14.3 Pivot Triggers (when GO becomes PIVOT)

  • If MARS schema freezes without SPARTA trust objects inside → pivot to Eurofighter LTE-first deployment to retain market option.
  • If Dassault forces a new-pillar framing → pivot to a smaller scope anchored on Pillar 3 Remote Carriers cyber-trust only.
  • If Indra sovereignty objection cannot be neutralized → pivot to a co-developed Spanish-anchored TSOC backend with SPARTA as the data contract authority.

14.4 No-Go Triggers

  • If Airbus DS Combat Cloud (Pillar 4) workshare is reduced or transferred between now and CDR.
  • If FCAS programme funding is materially reduced and demonstrator timeline slips beyond 2032.
  • If accreditation authorities reject the SPARTA authority-partition model in principle (not just on detail).
